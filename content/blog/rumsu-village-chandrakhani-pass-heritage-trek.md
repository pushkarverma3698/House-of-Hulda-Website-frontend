# The Ancient Trail to Chandrakhani Pass: Rumsu Village, Deodar Forests, and the Sanctuary of the Eighteen Gods

There are hikes that test your cardiovascular endurance, and then there are trails that walk you directly backward through human time. The trail from Rumsu village to **Chandrakhani Pass (3,660 meters)** is decidedly the latter.

When you step out of the heavy timber doorway of House of Hulda at dawn, you do not need to hire a shuttle or navigate highway traffic to reach the trailhead. The trailhead is simply the village path. It begins where the ancient slate-flagged courtyards of Rumsu end, threading past the carved deodar pillars of the local temple and climbing straight into an enchanted forest of deodar, horse chestnut, and weeping Himalayan spruce.

Above the tree line, the deodar gives way to rhododendron thickets, then to wide alpine meadows where Gaddi shepherds graze their flocks under the watchful gaze of Deo Tibba and Indrasan. And at the summit crest of the pass, where prayer flags snap against the biting winds, lies one of the most sacred mythological landscapes in the entire western Himalayas: the birthplace of Kullu Valley's divine order.

Whether you plan to hike Chandrakhani as an ambitious full-day alpine trek or spend slow days exploring the forested trails around our village, here is the complete insider's guide to this legendary Himalayan route.

## The Mythological Landscape: Jamlu Rishi and the Eighteen Gods

To walk the path to Chandrakhani without knowing its myth is to see only rock, soil, and pine. In the cultural memory of Himachal Pradesh—known historically as *Devbhoomi*, the Land of the Gods—Chandrakhani Pass is the pivot point of the valley's spiritual geography.

According to local Kullu folklore, the great sage **Jamlu Rishi** (the revered deity of the secretive village of Malana, situated just on the far side of the pass) was once carrying a sacred wicker basket (*pitara*) across the high ridge. The basket contained the images, essences, and spirits of the eighteen primary deities (*Athara Kardoo*) of the Himalayan highlands.

As Jamlu reached the windswept pass at 3,660 meters, a furious gale blew down from the snowfields of the Pir Panjal. The wind caught the basket and ripped it open. The eighteen deities tumbled out, scattering on the mountain gusts across the slopes, ravines, and riverbanks of the entire Kullu Valley. Wherever a deity landed, a village temple was consecrated, a sacred grove was protected, and an autonomous local deota took custody of the land and its people.

Even today, when villagers from Rumsu and Naggar hike across the pass, they pause at the crest to make an offering of a copper coin, a wildflower, or a small stone cairn. You are not merely standing on an alpine watershed; you are standing at the exact threshold where celestial mythology met mountain geography.

## Rumsu: The Living Kathkuni Village at 2,180m

House of Hulda is located in the upper hamlet of Rumsu, perched three kilometers above the historic town of [Naggar](/naggar). Unlike many roadside towns that have been altered by concrete and corrugated iron, Rumsu remains an authentic architectural open-air museum.

The village is constructed almost entirely in the traditional **Kathkuni** method: alternating layers of dry, hand-chiseled stone and massive interlocking deodar timber beams, assembled without a single ounce of cement or binding mortar. These structures breathe with the changing humidity of the seasons and possess natural seismic flexibility, having withstood massive Himalayan earthquakes over hundreds of years.

At the spiritual center of Rumsu stands the sacred temple of **Sharvan Devta**. The temple is an extraordinary showcase of woodcarving mastery:
- **Façade and Pillars**: Hand-carved reliefs depict coiled serpents (nagas), blooming lotus flowers, leaping deer, and guardian spirits.
- **Village Courtyard**: The open slate courtyard functions as the town square, where village elders gather in wool pattu coats to discuss seasonal harvests, and women spin raw sheep's wool using wooden spindles (*takli*).
- **Sacred Etiquette**: The inner sanctum of the village temple is fiercely protected. Visitors are welcome to admire the architecture and photography from the perimeter, but you must never touch the sacred wooden pillars or enter the inner courtyard while wearing leather belts, shoes, or bags. Always ask your local host at House of Hulda for guidance before wandering through the village sanctum.

## The Ascent to Chandrakhani Pass: Trail Breakdown

From the front deck of House of Hulda (2,180m) to the crest of Chandrakhani Pass (3,660m), you will cover approximately 14 kilometers round-trip, negotiating an elevation gain of **1,480 meters (4,855 feet)**. It is a demanding, deeply rewarding day-hike that showcases four distinct ecological zones.

### Stage 1: Rumsu Village to the Sterling Forest Clearing (2,600m)
- **Distance**: 2.5 km | **Time**: 1 to 1.5 hours
- **Terrain**: Gently climbing earthen path with stone steps, winding through dense, ancient deodar and brown oak groves.
- **The Experience**: You begin under the deep shade of old-growth conifers whose trunks measure over two meters in diameter. In early morning, sunlight filters through the pine boughs in dramatic golden shafts. You will pass natural mountain springs where cold, mineral-rich water bubbles out of stone spouts. Keep an ear out for the distinctive call of the Himalayan monal (Himachal's state bird) and the chatter of black-headed jays.
- **Rest Point**: Sterling is a serene grassy opening ringed by colossal cedar trees, often used by local herders as a grazing camp.

### Stage 2: The Rhododendron Forest to Chaklani Meadow (3,150m)
- **Distance**: 3.5 km | **Time**: 2 to 2.5 hours
- **Terrain**: Steeper, switchbacking trail through mixed birch and rhododendron forests, emerging onto wide, open sub-alpine grasslands.
- **The Experience**: As you climb past 2,800 meters, the deodar trees yield to gnarled Himalayan birch (*bhojpatra*) and dense thickets of pink and white rhododendron. By the time you reach Chaklani, the trees drop away entirely.
- **The View from Chaklani**: You are suddenly standing on a vast amphitheater of undulating alpine turf. Looking west across the Beas chasm, the snow-draped jagged horns of the Solang Valley and the Dhauladhar Range rise into sharp relief. During summer and autumn, Gaddi shepherds live in low, mortarless slate shelters here, accompanied by their fierce, collared Himalayan sheepdogs.

### Stage 3: The Alpine Ridge to Chandrakhani Crest (3,660m)
- **Distance**: 1.5 km | **Time**: 1 to 1.5 hours
- **Terrain**: Rocky, wind-swept ridgeline trail across broken shale, dwarf juniper bushes, and high-altitude scree.
- **The Experience**: The final climb follows the spine of the ridge. With every step, the horizon widens exponentially. The air is visibly thinner and carries the dry, icy scent of the glaciers.
- **The Summit Panorama**: Reaching the prayer-flagged cairn at 3,660 meters delivers an unforgettable visual crescendo. To the north and east loom the colossal, glaciated pyramids of **Deo Tibba (6,001m)** and **Indrasan (6,221m)**, glistening with hanging seracs and blue ice. Directly below you to the southeast lies the deep, mysterious, isolated gorge of Malana. Beyond stretch the endless, razor-sharp serrations of the Parvati Valley.

## Seasonal Guide: When to Walk the Trail

The character of Chandrakhani Pass changes dramatically with the turn of the seasons. Depending on your appetite for adventure, choose your window wisely:

### Spring & Early Summer (May to June): Wildflowers & High Snow
- **Trail Conditions**: Snow bridges linger in the shaded gullies above 3,000 meters. As the snow melts, Chaklani meadow turns into an iridescent carpet of alpine buttercups, purple primulas, and wild anemones.
- **Weather**: Mild daytime temperatures (15°C to 18°C on lower trails; 5°C to 8°C at the pass).
- **Verdict**: Ideal for photographers who love the contrast of lush green pastures against dramatic white snow peaks.

### Autumn (September to November): Crystalline Skies & Golden Grass
- **Trail Conditions**: The entire trail is bone-dry and stable. The monsoon clouds have cleared completely, leaving the atmosphere with near-infinite visibility. The alpine grasses at Chaklani turn an incandescent shade of amber gold.
- **Weather**: Crisp, sharp, and invigorating. Temperatures at the pass can hover near freezing, with brisk summit winds.
- **Verdict**: The single finest season for trekking. The line-of-sight to the 6,000m peaks is flawless, with zero haze and stable weather.

### Winter (December to April): The Realm of Snowshoes & Frost
- **Trail Conditions**: Deep snow envelopes the trail starting from just above Rumsu village. Reaching the pass requires winter mountaineering gear, gaiters, and crampons.
- **Verdict**: Unless accompanied by a certified alpine winter mountaineer, casual day-hikers should turn around at Sterling clearing (2,600m), which offers a winter wonderland hike without objective avalanche risk.

## Practical Hiking Guide: Logistics, Trailhead & Gear

A day-hike to 3,660 meters demands preparation and respect for mountain weather. Use this checklist to plan your trek:

### How to Reach the Trailhead
- **Basecamp**: Stay at House of Hulda in Rumsu. Starting your trek from our doorstep allows you to begin walking at 6:30 AM without needing to arrange early morning mountain transport.
- **Access to Rumsu**: From Delhi or Chandigarh, take the overnight bus to Patlikuhl, then a 20-minute taxi up through Naggar to Rumsu village. If arriving via Bhuntar Airport, the private cab drive takes about 75 minutes.

### What to Pack in Your Daypack
- **Water & Electrolytes**: Carry at least 2 liters of water per person. There are reliable natural springs between Rumsu and Sterling, but water sources dry up on the high ridge past Chaklani.
- **Footwear**: Sturdy hiking boots or trail running shoes with aggressive Vibram-style lugged soles. The shale along the upper ridge requires good ankle support and grip.
- **Layering System**: High-altitude weather shifts quickly. Pack a lightweight fleece mid-layer, a windproof and waterproof hooded shell jacket, lightweight gloves, and a UV-rated sun hat.
- **Sun Protection**: UV radiation at 3,660m is intense. Pack polarized sunglasses (category 3 or 4) and mineral sunscreen (SPF 50+).
- **Trail Nourishment**: Pack energy-dense trail snacks—almonds, dried figs, energy bars, and crisp local orchard apples from our kitchen.

### Altitude Preparation & Acclimatization Strategy
- **Pacing Over Speed**: Climbing 1,480 vertical meters in a single morning is a serious athletic endeavor. Maintain a steady, rhythmic "mountaineer's pace"—a slow, measured cadence that allows you to breathe comfortably without gasping.
- **Acclimatization Days**: Do not attempt Chandrakhani on your first day in the valley. Spend your first day relaxing at House of Hulda at 2,180m, wandering the quiet orchard paths or visiting the [Roerich Art Gallery and Naggar Castle](/naggar). This lets your blood chemistry adjust naturally to the lower partial pressure of oxygen.
- **Turnaround Time**: Establish a strict turnaround deadline. If you have not reached the pass by 1:30 PM, turn back regardless of how close you feel. Mountain weather often clouds over in the late afternoon, and you will want to be safely below the tree line well before dusk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can Chandrakhani Pass be completed as a single-day hike from Rumsu?
Yes. Fit hikers who begin early (by 6:30 AM to 7:00 AM) can reach the pass in 4 to 5 hours, spend 45 minutes soaking in the summit panorama, and descend back to Rumsu village in 3 to 4 hours, arriving before sundown.

### Do I need a local trekking guide?
For first-time mountain hikers, hiring a local village guide from Rumsu is strongly recommended. While the trail through the forest is relatively well-defined, navigating the ridge above Chaklani when afternoon mist rolls in can be disorienting. Our team at House of Hulda can arrange an experienced local guide from our village family.

### Can I cross over Chandrakhani Pass to visit the village of Malana?
Historically, this trail was the ancient trade route connecting the Kullu Valley to Malana. It is possible to trek across the pass and descend into Malana (an additional 3 to 4 hours from the pass). However, Malana is an exceptionally orthodox cultural community with very strict rules regarding outside visitors. Most travelers prefer doing a round-trip day hike back to the comfortable warmth of Rumsu.

### Is the trail suitable for children or older travelers?
The full summit hike to 3,660 meters is physically demanding. However, the first section—walking from House of Hulda up to Sterling forest clearing (2,600m)—is a gentle, safe, and utterly magical 2-hour forest walk suitable for families, children, and casual wanderers.

### Are there food stalls or teashops along the trail?
During peak summer (May and June), seasonal shepherd teashops may operate at Sterling and Chaklani. However, they are not guaranteed. Always carry your own water, lunch, and energy provisions packed fresh from our kitchen.
